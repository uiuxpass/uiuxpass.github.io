Touch target size: 24 px, 44 pt and 48 dp explained

5 min read · Updated 23 November 2026

Three numbers get quoted for tap targets, and they are all correct, because they come from different rules with different jobs. Here is what each one means and which to design to.

The three rules

SourceSizeWhat it is
WCAG 2.2, 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), level AA24 × 24 CSS pxThe legal-style floor. Smaller targets pass only if they have enough space around them, or there is another way to do the same thing.
WCAG 2.2, 2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced), level AAA44 × 44 CSS pxThe comfortable size for everyone, including people with tremor.
Apple Human Interface Guidelines44 × 44 ptRecommended minimum for iOS controls.
Google Material Design48 × 48 dpRecommended minimum for Android, with about 8 dp between targets.

In practice: design to 44 px or more on touch screens, and treat 24 px as the line you must never cross. A pt, a dp and a CSS px are all roughly the same physical size on modern phones.

The target is not the icon

A 20 px icon can sit in a 44 px target. The tappable area is what counts, so add padding, not a bigger icon. The most common failures are:

  • Icon-only buttons (close ✕, more ⋯, edit) sized to the icon.
  • Text links inside paragraphs on mobile, especially when two sit on adjacent lines.
  • Checkboxes and radio buttons where only the box is clickable, not the label.
  • Pagination dots and carousel arrows.
  • Table row actions crammed into a narrow column.

How to check a page in a minute

  1. Open the page at a phone width (390 px is a good default).
  2. List every clickable element and measure its box, not its visible shape.
  3. Flag anything under 44 px, and anything under 24 px with less than 24 px of clear space around it as a hard failure.
  4. Also check spacing: two 44 px targets touching each other still cause mis-taps.

Doing step 2 by hand on a real page means dozens of elements. A script that reads the rendered layout does it in seconds and does not skip the ones you forgot existed.
