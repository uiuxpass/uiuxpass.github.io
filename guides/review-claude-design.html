How to review a design Claude made: a 10-minute checklist

6 min read · Updated 23 November 2026

AI tools now build a working page from one prompt. They are fast and often good, and they consistently miss the same things. This checklist takes about ten minutes and catches most of them before anyone else sees the page.

The checklist

MinuteCheckWhat AI usually gets wrong
1–2Contrast of body text, grey labels and buttonsLight greys around 2.5:1 and pastel buttons with white text. Body text needs 4.5:1.
3Press Tab through the pageNo visible focus ring, or focus disappears into custom components.
4–5Resize to 390 px wideSideways scrolling, tables that overflow, text that collides, buttons under 44 px.
6Empty, loading and error statesOnly the happy path exists. Ask what the page shows with no data and when a request fails.
7Real contentPlaceholder names, round made-up numbers, fake testimonials. Replace or remove them.
8MotionAnimations on everything, no reduced-motion fallback.
9–10The generic AI lookEmoji icons, purple-to-blue gradient hero, three identical feature cards, a pill label above the heading, exactly two buttons.

Why "looks fine" is not enough

We ran 66 automated browser checks on a typical pricing page made by AI in one prompt. It passed 38. The failures were not visible in a screenshot: focus that vanished, contrast just under the line, a table that overflowed only at phone width. After fixing exactly what the checks reported, it passed 66 of 66, and it looked noticeably less like a template.

Prompts that make the AI check itself

  • "List every text colour and background on this page with its contrast ratio. Fix anything under 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for icons and borders."
  • "Show me the empty, loading and error state for each section."
  • "Name the three things that make this page look like a template and replace them with something specific to this product."

Prompts help, but the model still grades its own work. A script that measures the page and reports a number it cannot argue with is the step that makes the result trustworthy.
