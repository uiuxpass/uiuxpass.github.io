How to write error messages that tell people how to fix the problem

6 min read · Updated 24 November 2026

An error message has one job: get the person moving again. "Invalid input" tells them something went wrong and nothing else. They have to guess what, guess why and try again. A good error message removes the guessing.

The formula: what happened, then how to fix it

Most good error messages have two parts, sometimes three:

  1. What happened, in the person's words: "This card number is too short."
  2. How to fix it, as a clear action: "Enter the full number from the front of your card."
  3. Why, only when it helps them decide: "We need this to send your receipt."

If you can only keep one part, keep the fix. "Enter a date after today" works without "Invalid date" in front of it.

An error code is fine as a last line for support. It is never the whole message.

Six rules for the words

  • Do not blame. "You entered the wrong date" becomes "Enter a date after today".
  • Be specific. Name the field and the rule. "Something went wrong" is only for errors you truly cannot explain.
  • Use plain words. Not "authentication token expired" but "You've been signed out. Sign in again to continue."
  • Keep it short. One or two sentences. People scan error text; they do not read it.
  • No jokes, no "Oops". Humour lands badly when someone has just lost their work.
  • Match the button. If the fix is an action, the button says it: "Try again", "Use another card".

Where and when to show errors

  • Next to the field, directly below it, not only in a banner at the top.
  • After the person leaves the field, or on submit. Showing "Invalid email" while they are still typing the first letters is noise.
  • On submit with several errors, show a short summary at the top that links to each field, and move keyboard focus to it.
  • Keep what they typed. Never clear a form because one field failed.
  • Show rules before the error. Password and format rules belong under the label from the start, as visible hint text that stays while they type.
  • Remove the error as soon as it is fixed, so the screen confirms they got it right.

Accessibility rules that apply

WCAG 2.2 criterionLevelWhat it means for errors
3.3.1 Error IdentificationAThe field in error is identified and the error is described in text.
3.3.3 Error SuggestionAAWhen a fix is known, the message suggests it.
1.4.1 Use of ColorAA red border alone is not enough. Add text and an icon.

3.3.4 Error Prevention (Legal, Financial, Data)AAFor payments, legal commitments and data changes, people can review, confirm or undo before it is final.

In code, connect the message to its field with aria-describedby and mark the field with aria-invalid="true", so a screen reader reads the error when the field gets focus.
