Is my A/B test result real? A plain guide to significance and sample size

6 min read · Updated 23 November 2026

Variant B converted at 3.75% and A at 3.00%. That is a 25% lift. Ship it? Not yet. Here is how to tell a real result from noise, in plain words.

A worked example

A: 120 sign-ups from 4,000 visitors   3.00%
B: 150 sign-ups from 4,000 visitors   3.75%
Two-proportion test: z = 1.86, p = 0.063

A p-value of 0.063 means: if A and B were truly the same, you would see a gap this big about 6 times in 100 tests. The usual bar is 5 in 100 (p < 0.05), so this result is promising but not proven. The right call is to keep running to the sample size you planned, not to ship and not to give up.

How many visitors you need

Visitors needed per variant to reliably detect a 20% relative lift (two-sided test, 5% significance, 80% power):

Current conversionLift you want to detectVisitors per variant
3%3% → 3.6%about 13,900
5%5% → 6%about 8,200
10%10% → 12%about 3,800

Low conversion rates and small lifts need a lot of traffic. If you have 500 visitors a week, a test hunting for a 20% lift on a 3% rate would take over a year. In that case, test bigger changes or use qualitative research instead.

Five mistakes that fake a result

  1. Peeking and stopping early. Checking every day and stopping the moment p dips under 0.05 can multiply false wins several times over. Decide the sample size first.
  2. Uneven split. If a 50/50 test ends at 52/48 with large numbers, something in the setup is broken (a sample-ratio mismatch). Fix it before reading results.
  3. Too many metrics. Test 10 metrics and one will "win" by chance. Pick one primary metric up front, or correct for multiple comparisons.
  4. Too short. Run at least one full week so weekday and weekend visitors both count.
  5. Reporting only the lift. Report the range: "+25% (95% range roughly −1% to +58%)" tells the truth; "+25%" does not.
