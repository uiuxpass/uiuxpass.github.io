Before you ship an AI-built interface: a final check in five areas

9 min read · Updated 21 November 2026

AI tools now produce a working page in a minute. They also produce the same page: purple-to-blue gradient, emoji icons, glowing cards, a placeholder sentence someone forgot, and a "Submit" button that is actually a <span>. The first draft is fast. The check after it is where it becomes a product.

We ran a typical AI-made pricing page through 66 automated browser checks. It passed 38. Here is what failed, grouped into the five areas worth checking on every AI-built interface, and how to fix each.

1. Accessibility: the failures that lock people out

Seven critical failures on our test page, all common in generated code:

  • Text contrast: a "Submit" button label at 2.82:1 and a "/mo" label at 4.18:1. Body text needs 4.5:1.
  • Fake buttons: clickable <span>s for the monthly/yearly toggle. They cannot be reached with Tab and are invisible to screen readers. Use <button>.
  • Blocked zoom: maximum-scale=1 in the viewport tag stops people from enlarging text. Remove it.
  • Small targets: icon buttons under 44 px on mobile.
  • Ignored reduced motion: animations that run even when the user asked the system for less motion.

2. Layout: what breaks at 390 px

Generated layouts are usually designed at one desktop width. At 390 px, the three fixed-width cards ran off the screen; at 320 px the page was 740 px wider than the viewport. Check:

  • No horizontal scrolling at 390 px (and at 320 px for WCAG reflow).
  • No text cut off by its box, no overlapping controls.
  • Layout shift under 0.1 while the page loads, so buttons don't jump under the cursor.

3. Typography: hierarchy and readability

  • Body text at least 16 px on mobile inputs (smaller makes iOS zoom in).
  • Lines under about 90 characters, line height 1.3–2.0.
  • The largest heading at least 1.5 times the body size, so the page has a clear entry point.
  • No more than three font families and about twelve sizes.
  • Tabular numbers for prices and metrics, so digits line up.

4. Motion: fast, purposeful, interruptible

  • UI motion under 300 ms (dialogs and drawers up to 500 ms).
  • Ease-out for things entering, never ease-in.
  • Animate transform and opacity only; never width, top or margin.
  • No transition: all, no endless animations except loaders, nothing appearing from scale(0).

5. Surface: the generic AI look

These are not bugs, but they make a product look generated and interchangeable:

  • Default purple-blue gradients and gradient-filled text (unless they are your brand).
  • Coloured glow shadows and cards nested inside cards.
  • Emoji used as icons and a rounded icon tile above every heading.
  • More than five corner radii or shadow styles on one page.

And the content checks that embarrass launches: placeholder Latin or to-do notes left in the copy, links to #, JavaScript and console errors, failed requests and broken images.

The result

Fixing exactly what the checks found, with the content unchanged, took the page from 38 to 66 of 66: readable contrast, real buttons, zoom allowed, a layout that fits a phone, calmer surfaces. The first hand-fix still left two critical items (a button at 4.23:1 and a 40 px toggle); measuring again caught them. That is the point of checking again: you check the fix too.

Make it routine

  1. Run the checks at 1440 and 390 px on every page an AI tool produces.
  2. Fix critical items first (they block people), then major, then the generic look.
  3. Run the checks again. Ship when critical is zero.
