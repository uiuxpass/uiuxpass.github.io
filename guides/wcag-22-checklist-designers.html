WCAG 2.2 AA checklist for designers: what you can check in the design file

7 min read · Updated 24 November 2026

WCAG 2.2 Level AA has 55 success criteria: 31 at Level A and 24 at Level AA. Many of them are decided in the design file, long before a developer writes a line. Fixing contrast or a missing error state in Figma takes minutes; fixing it after release takes a ticket, a review and a deploy.

This checklist covers the criteria a designer can check on a static frame, and marks the ones that only a test of the built page can confirm.

Check these on every frame

CriterionLevelWhat to check in the design
1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)AABody text at least 4.5:1 against its background. Large text (24 px regular, or about 18.7 px bold) at least 3:1.
1.4.11 Non-text ContrastAAInput borders, checkboxes, icons that carry meaning and chart lines at least 3:1 against what is next to them.
1.4.1 Use of ColorAErrors, required fields, links in text and chart series are not told apart by colour alone. Add an icon, text, underline or pattern.
2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)AAEvery tap or click target at least 24 × 24 CSS px, or spaced so a 24 px circle centred on it touches no other target.
2.4.7 Focus VisibleAAA focus style is designed for every interactive component, not only buttons.
2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum)AASticky headers, cookie bars and chat bubbles cannot fully cover a focused element.
1.4.10 ReflowAAA frame at 320 px wide exists and needs no sideways scrolling, except for content such as data tables and maps.
1.3.1 Info and RelationshipsAHeadings look like headings and follow one order. Each field has a visible label tied to it.
2.4.6 Headings and LabelsAAHeadings and labels say what the section or field is about.
3.3.2 Labels or InstructionsAFormat rules (date format, password rules) are shown before the person types, and stay visible while they type.
3.3.1 Error IdentificationAEvery error state names the field and the problem in text.
3.3.3 Error SuggestionAAEvery error says how to fix it, when a fix is known.
1.4.13 Content on Hover or FocusAATooltips and popovers can be dismissed, can be hovered over, and stay until the person moves away.
2.2.2 Pause, Stop, HideACarousels and anything that moves for more than 5 seconds have a visible pause control.
1.1.1 Non-text ContentAEach image and icon is marked as meaningful (write its alt text in the handoff notes) or decorative.

The six new A and AA criteria in WCAG 2.2

WCAG 2.2 added nine success criteria. Three of them are AAA. The other six are part of any AA audit, and most are design decisions:

CriterionLevelDesign decision
2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum)AALeave room for focus around sticky bars, or make them push content instead of covering it.
2.5.7 Dragging MovementsAAEvery drag (reorder, slider, map pan) has a single-tap alternative, such as up and down buttons or a number field.
2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)AA24 × 24 px minimum, or enough spacing. Platform guidance is larger: 44 pt on iOS and 48 dp on Android.
3.2.6 Consistent HelpAIf help (contact link, chat, phone) appears on several pages, it sits in the same relative place on each.
3.3.7 Redundant EntryADo not ask for the same information twice in one process. Pre-fill it or offer "same as billing address".
3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum)AASign-in does not depend on remembering or transcribing. Allow paste and password managers; avoid puzzle tests without an alternative.

WCAG 2.2 also removed 4.1.1 Parsing, which was about HTML syntax and never a design concern.

Contrast numbers worth knowing

Most failures in AI-made and quickly made screens are light grey text. These ratios are against white (#FFFFFF), calculated with the WCAG formula:

ColourRatio on whiteBody textLarge text and UI parts
#7676764.54:1PassesPasses
#6B72804.83:1PassesPasses
#8B8B8B3.41:1FailsPasses
#9CA3AF2.54:1FailsFails
#D1D5DB (common input border)1.47:1FailsFails

#767676 is the lightest neutral grey that passes 4.5:1 on white. A light border such as #D1D5DB is fine for a decorative divider, but not as the only edge of a text field.

What the design file cannot prove

Some criteria depend on the built page: keyboard order and keyboard traps, screen reader names, zoom to 200%, text spacing overrides, status messages announced without focus, and whether labels in code match the visible label. Write the intent in your handoff notes (tab order, alt text, what a screen reader should announce), then test the build. An automated scan finds a share of issues; a person with a keyboard and a screen reader finds the rest.
